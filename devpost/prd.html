PRD review

User Story & Acceptance Criteria Assistant

A lightweight agile assistant that turns informal business requests into a backlog-ready story, measurable Gherkin acceptance criteria, and a clear dependency/open-question list for refinement. 
flowchart LR
    A[Raw business request] --> B[Input screen]
    B --> C[Generate]
    C --> D[User story]
    C --> E[Gherkin criteria]
    C --> F[Dependencies / open questions]
    D --> G[Copy Markdown]
    E --> G
    F --> G

Core journey

  • Open the app and paste a request.
  • Choose a role if useful.
  • Generate the structured artifact.
  • Review output and copy it to a backlog tool.

What the user sees

  • One input surface for the raw requirement.
  • One output card with story, criteria, and questions.
  • Visible copy action for Markdown.

Proof of success

  • A vague request becomes a structured story.
  • Acceptance criteria are testable in Gherkin.
  • Hidden dependencies and missing facts are surfaced.
Scope said: “turn raw requests into structured backlog artifacts”

Now the behavior is explicit: the app accepts freeform text, produces a user story, creates Gherkin acceptance criteria, and highlights technical dependencies or unanswered questions before the team estimates or refines the work.

What if the request is vague or incomplete?

The app should still produce a best-effort result, but it must clearly label missing information and unresolved assumptions instead of pretending the requirement is fully ready.

What stays out of scope for now?

Authentication, persistence, direct Jira/Azure DevOps integration, multi-user collaboration, and advanced backlog history are intentionally deferred. They are useful later, but not needed to prove the core idea in this MVP.

This PRD is intentionally small and proof-of-concept sized. It defines what the user experiences and how success is judged without drifting into architecture or implementation specifics.
